# Lesson 3: Generating Your First AI Video Clips

**Goal:** Generate 3–4 usable clips from your lesson 2 script using a mainstream text-to-video tool.
**Video length:** ~18 minutes

---

## Audio narration script

Today is the most fun lesson — and the most dangerous, because generating is addictive. The rule is simple: we generate with a plan. Your plan is the script you wrote in lesson two.

Section 1. The workflow is the same everywhere. Runway, Pika, Kling, Veo — different buttons, the same shape: prompt in, clip out. Pick one tool for this lesson. Every skill you learn today transfers to all the others.

Section 2. Walkthrough — text to video. [demo] Open your tool. Copy shot one from your lesson two script into the prompt box. Set the aspect ratio to vertical — nine by sixteen — because TikTok, Reels, and Shorts are vertical. Set the length to five seconds. Generate. Watch. Keep it or regenerate. That is the whole loop: prompt, settings, generate, decide.

Section 3. Iterate twice — do not write one perfect prompt. Your first generation is a draft, not a verdict. Change one thing — the camera move, the lighting — and generate again. [show the same prompt run twice with one change] Two short iterations beat one long perfect prompt every time, because the model needs a chance to show you what it can do. A fifty-word prompt is just a guess. Two ten-word prompts are a conversation.

Section 4. The twenty percent rule. Expect a twenty percent keeper rate. Generate ten clips, keep two. That is normal — it is not failure, it is the craft. Professionals generate in batches and curate ruthlessly. [show a batch of generations, keepers being moved to a folder]

Section 5. Image to video — the consistency cheat. Generate one strong first frame — or grab a frame from your best clip — and let the tool animate it. Your subject stays consistent across shots because the tool is working from the same image every time. This is how you keep one hero product or person looking like themselves across a whole video. [demo]

Section 6. Generate your three to four clips. Work through your script one shot at a time. Download your keepers immediately — free tiers delete old generations. And name your files like a professional: project name, shot number, tool. For example: restaurant-shot1-runway.mp4. Future you will be grateful.

Section 7. Free tier limits. You will hit limits on free tiers — that is normal, not a wall. When you do, switch tools. That is why this course teaches you the workflow instead of one product: there are always at least two tools with free credits at any given moment.

Wrap up. Here is your homework: generate at least eight clips from your lesson two script. Keep your best three or four — one per shot. Put them in a folder called "my first film". In the next lesson, we add a voice and captions, and your clips will start sounding like a real video.
