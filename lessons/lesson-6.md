# Lesson 6: Getting Paid for This Skill

**Goal:** Know the three realistic ways to earn from AI video creation — and how to price a first client project so it is easy to say yes to.
**Video length:** ~15 minutes

---

## Audio narration script

Everything so far was practice. This lesson is the payoff — how to turn the skill you just learned into income.

Section 1. Path one — your own content. Post your films consistently and monetize the views: creator programs, ads, sponsorships. Your course project is your first portfolio piece — publish it publicly this week, not when it is perfect. Perfect is a moving target; published is a fact.

Section 2. Path two — local businesses. Restaurants, salons, churches, shops: everyone needs social content, and nobody has time to make it. The move is the demo-first pitch: make one free sample video for a local business, show it to them, then offer a monthly package. Do not sell "posts" — sell a package. Four to eight videos a month, delivered on a schedule. A package is easy to say yes to, easy to deliver, and it pays every month. [show a sample pitch message on screen]

Section 3. Path three — freelance platforms. Fiverr, Upwork, Twitter, LinkedIn. Start with a narrow offer: "I make thirty-second AI videos for restaurants." Narrow beats general every time, because a specific offer is easy to say yes to and easy to find. And remember: your first three jobs are for reviews, not riches. Deliver fast, over-communicate, ask for a review, and raise your price.

Section 4. Pricing your first client project. Do not charge hourly — charge per video or per package. First-client pricing should be low enough to say yes to and high enough to respect: fifteen to thirty thousand naira per video, or forty to sixty thousand naira a month for a package. Raise your price every three to five clients. And always — always — get fifty percent upfront. The client who pays fifty percent upfront is the client who respects your time.

Section 5. The wrap. You now have the full pipeline: script, generate, voice, captions, edit, get paid. The tools will change — new names, new buttons, new prices. The pipeline will not. Learn the pipeline once, and you can use any tool that comes next.

Your homework is simple and it matters more than any other exercise in this course: post your finished film publicly, and send your pitch message to three local businesses this week. That is the whole course. Now go make something.

## Quiz

**Question 1:** What is the "demo-first pitch"?
A. Charging a client before showing any work
B. Making one free sample video for a business, then offering a package
C. Sending a price list to every business in town
D. Posting your portfolio and waiting for clients

**Question 2:** What should you sell to a local business instead of single "posts"?
A. A one-off video
B. A monthly package of 4–8 videos
C. A subscription to your own channel
D. A camera

**Question 3:** Why start with a narrow freelance offer like "30-second AI videos for restaurants"?
A. It is easier to say yes to and easier to find
B. It charges more than a general offer
C. It requires less skill
D. It avoids the need for reviews

**Question 4:** What is a realistic first-client price for one video?
A. ₦500
B. ₦15,000–₦30,000

**Question 5:** How much of the payment should you get upfront, always?
A. Nothing — invoice on completion

## Exercise

1. **Post your finished film publicly** — TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, or X. It does not need to be perfect; it needs to be published.
2. Write a pitch message (3–4 sentences: who you are, what you made for them, what you offer, what it costs).
3. Send it to **3 local businesses** this week — a restaurant, a salon, a shop, a church, anyone with something to sell.
4. Record your results in a notebook: who replied, who said no, what you will change next time.
