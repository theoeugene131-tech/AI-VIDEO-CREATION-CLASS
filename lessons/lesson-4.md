# Lesson 4: Adding a Voice and Captions

**Goal:** Generate a natural AI voiceover with ElevenLabs and add clean auto-captions in CapCut.
**Video length:** ~15 minutes

---

## Audio narration script

Silent videos get watched. Narrated, captioned videos get remembered. Today we add both — and it is easier than you think.

Section 1. Write a voiceover that fits AI visuals. Rule one: five to seven words per sentence. One idea per sentence. AI clips are short, so your sentences must be too. Rule two: a thirty-second video is only sixty to seventy-five words. That is four or five sentences — nothing more. Rule three: read it aloud. If you run out of breath, split the sentence. Here is the voiceover for our restaurant film, and you can see it on screen: "Some places feed you. This place welcomes you. Jollof, cooked slow, served fast. Adunni's Kitchen. Come hungry." Count it — twenty-two words, four sentences, and it fits thirty seconds perfectly.

Section 2. ElevenLabs — generate the voiceover. [demo] Open ElevenLabs and choose Text to Speech. Paste your script. Pick a voice — or clone your own voice, which only ever do with your own voice or someone's with written consent. One setting matters most: stability. Lower means more emotional and expressive; higher means steadier and calmer. For beginners, keep it in the middle. Generate in chunks — one paragraph at a time — and keep the takes you like. [generate two takes, compare]

Section 3. The budget alternative. You can skip ElevenLabs entirely and record your own voice on your phone in a quiet room. Honest truth: for a personal brand, your own voice always wins, because nobody can clone you. AI voices are for clients, for testing, and for when you need a voice you do not have.

Section 4. Captions are non-negotiable. Most people watch social video with the sound off first. If you have no captions, you lose them before the first second ends. Captions are not an optional extra — they are part of the video.

Section 5. Auto-captions in CapCut. [demo] Import your voiceover, tap Auto Captions, and generate. Then proofread every single line — AI captions murder names and slang. [show errors live and fix them] Now style them: three to four words per line, a high-contrast outline, and keep them inside the safe margins so the platform's interface does not cover them.

Section 6. Sync voice to pictures. Lay your clips on the timeline, drag the voiceover in, and cut the picture when the sentence changes. The ear leads the eye — every new sentence is a new shot. When the words change and the picture changes at the same moment, the video feels alive.

Wrap up. Here is your homework: record or generate a thirty-second voiceover for your film, add auto-captions in CapCut, proofread every line, and style them for vertical video. In the next lesson, we edit everything together — and this is where it starts looking like a real video.

## Exercise

1. Write a 30-second voiceover (60–75 words, 5–7 words per sentence) for your film.
2. Generate it with ElevenLabs (or record your own voice on your phone in a quiet room).
3. Import it into CapCut, generate auto-captions, and **proofread every line**.
4. Style the captions: 3–4 words per line, high contrast, inside the safe margins.
5. Save the project as `my-first-film` in CapCut.
