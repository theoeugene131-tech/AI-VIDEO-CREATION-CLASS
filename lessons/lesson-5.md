# Lesson 5: Editing and Polishing

**Goal:** Stitch clips, voiceover, and captions into a polished 30–60 second video — exported for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.
**Video length:** ~18 minutes

---

## Audio narration script

This is the lesson that separates an AI experiment from an actual video. CapCut is free, and it does everything a beginner needs.

Section 1. The workspace. [demo] Import your clips, your voiceover, and a music track. Here is the timeline: clips on top, voiceover on an audio track, music on a lower track. That is the whole layout — everything else is detail.

Section 2. Pacing — cut to the beat. Cut on the sentence beats you learned in lesson four. Remove dead air. Tighten every cut, because attention is currency. Then learn two professional tricks. The J-cut: the next clip's audio starts a beat before the picture changes. The L-cut: the current clip's audio rolls over the next shot. Together they make your edits feel invisible — viewers stop noticing the cuts and start watching the story. [demo both]

Section 3. Title card. Two to three seconds at the very start: what the video is about, in words a phone screen can read. If viewers cannot tell what you offer in three seconds, they scroll. [show title card examples]

Section 4. Transitions that do not look cheap. Use match cuts and speed ramps. Avoid star wipes, lightning bolts, and anything that screams 2012. One transition style per video, maximum. The best transition is usually no transition at all — just a well-timed cut.

Section 5. Color — the consistency tell. Apply one color grade across every clip. When shot one is warm and shot two is cold, viewers think "AI" before they think anything else. Same grade, same film. [demo: before/after grade]

Section 6. Music and sound. Use royalty-free music — the CapCut library, the YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay. Then duck it: lower the music twelve to eighteen decibels under the voiceover so the speech stays clear. Add subtle sound effects between shots — a soft whoosh, a gentle pop — but less is more. [demo]

Section 7. Export for each platform. [demo] TikTok, Reels, Shorts: 1080 by 1920 — vertical, nine by sixteen — at thirty frames per second. YouTube and Facebook feed: 1920 by 1080, or 1080 by 1080 for square. Always export at the highest quality and let the platform compress it — never compress twice.

Wrap up. Here is your homework: edit your film. Five clips, voiceover, captions, title card, music, one grade. Export in nine by sixteen. It should be thirty to sixty seconds long. In the final lesson, we talk about getting paid for what you just learned.

## Exercise

1. Open CapCut and start a new project called `my-first-film`.
2. Assemble your film: 5 clips + voiceover + captions + title card + music + one color grade.
3. Cut every shot to a sentence beat; use at least one J-cut or L-cut.
4. Duck the music 12–18dB under the voiceover.
5. Export in 9:16 (1080x1920, 30fps). Your film should be 30–60 seconds long.
