# Lesson 1: What You Need to Get Started

**Goal:** Understand the four parts of every AI video, set up every tool, and generate your first test clip.
**Video length:** ~15 minutes

---

## Audio narration script

[record this nearly word-for-word; [bracketed] lines are stage directions, not spoken]

Cold open. By the end of this course, you will have made a complete AI video — visuals, voice, captions, and music — without a camera, without an editing degree, and without spending more than a few thousand naira. I am [your name], and this is lesson one: what you need to get started.

Section 1. What this course covers — and what it does not. Let us set expectations honestly. This course covers the complete pipeline: taking an idea, writing a script an AI can use, generating video clips, adding a voice and captions, editing everything together, and getting paid for the skill. It does not cover filmmaking theory, advanced visual effects, or every button in every tool. And here is the most important thing to understand: this space moves fast. Tools change names, prices, and interfaces every few months. That is why, in lesson two, I will teach you how to evaluate any tool — not just the ones I show you today. If you understand the pipeline, you can use any tool.

Section 2. The four things every AI video needs. Every AI video you will ever make — yours or a client's — is built from exactly four things. Number one: a script. A short plan of what happens, shot by shot. Number two: generated visuals. The clips themselves, created by AI video tools from your script. Number three: a voice. A narration that ties the clips together and tells the viewer what to feel and what to do. Number four: an editor. The free software where clips, voice, captions, and music become one finished video. That is the whole pipeline: script, visuals, voice, editor. Remember those four things — every lesson in this course is one of them.

Section 3. Why you do not need a camera, a studio, or coding skill. The visuals are generated by AI from text. The voice is generated — or recorded on your phone. The editing happens in a free app with drag and drop. There is no filming, no lighting setup, no code. If you have a laptop, or even just a phone, and an internet connection, you have everything you need to start today.

Section 4. The tools tour. Let me introduce the tools we will use. First, the text-to-video tools: Runway, Pika, Kling, and Google's Veo. You type words describing a shot, and they generate a short video clip. They are your camera crew — fast and cheap, but they need clear instructions. Second, ElevenLabs: this is your voice studio. It generates realistic narration, and it can clone a voice — including yours — with permission. Third, CapCut: a free editor for your phone or computer where everything comes together. Clips in, finished video out. All of these have free tiers, which means you can follow this entire course without paying anything. Realistic budget: free tiers are enough to learn; roughly ten to twenty-five thousand naira a month, about seven to fifteen dollars, unlocks serious output once you start taking clients.

Section 5. How to evaluate any tool. Since tools change, here is the checklist I use for any new AI video tool. One: can it turn text into video? Two: can it animate an image into video? Three: can you control the aspect ratio and the length? Four: does it have a free tier or trial? If a tool checks those four boxes, the workflow you are learning today works inside it — whatever it is called.

Section 6. Set up your accounts. Pause this video now and create an account on each tool: Runway, Pika or Kling, ElevenLabs, and CapCut. Use the same email for all of them. It takes ten minutes. Do it now — not after the course. [pause]

Section 7. Your first generation. Once your accounts are ready, open any text-to-video tool and type this exact prompt: "A plate of jollof rice on a wooden table, steam rising, slow push-in, warm cinematic light." Set the format to vertical — nine by sixteen — generate, and watch. It will not be perfect. That is the point. You have just seen the raw material that everything else in this course is built from.

Wrap up. Here is your homework. One: finish creating your accounts. Two: generate three test clips — one each in two different tools — and save your best one. Three: keep that best clip somewhere safe. By the end of this course you will compare it to the video you make, and the difference will motivate you. In the next lesson, we take on the most important skill in this course: writing a script that an AI can actually use. See you there.

2. In two different video tools, generate one test clip each using any prompt you like.
3. Save your best clip in a folder called `my-course-work`. You will compare it to your final project at the end of the course.
